Built for Pennsylvania winters

Why the base is the job

Southeastern Pennsylvania freezes and thaws dozens of times each winter, and every cycle tries to lift, tip and separate whatever sits on the ground. A patio that lasts here is built from the bottom up: soil excavated, modified stone base compacted in lifts, bedding screeded flat, pavers set tight, joints locked with polymeric sand. Skip a step and the surface tells on the builder within two winters.

That base stone comes off our own trucks, the same 3/4 inch modified we sell by the ton on the delivery page. And digging starts only after utility lines are marked through PA One Call (811).

The pretty part is the last ten percent. The other ninety is dirt, stone and compaction, and that's where a patio is won.

Good questions

Hardscaping, asked and answered

How much does a paver patio cost?

Size, material, access and site prep drive the number, so we don't guess it over the phone. We walk the yard, measure it, and put a fair number in writing. The walk-through and the estimate are free.

How long does a patio or walkway take?

Smaller walkways can be a matter of days; bigger patios take longer. Your quote comes with a timeline, and the site is left clean every night in between.

Pavers or poured concrete?

Both have their place. Around here, pavers handle freeze-thaw movement better: joints flex where a slab cracks, and a settled paver can be lifted and reset instead of ripped out. Concrete still wins for some pads. We'll tell you straight which fits your project.

Can you repair an existing patio or wall?

Often, yes. Settled corners, heaved steps and leaning walls can frequently be rebuilt on a corrected base rather than replaced whole. Send a photo with your estimate request and we'll take a look.
